# The Effect of Nitrogen on the Localized Corrosion Resistance of Duplex Stainless Steel Simulated Weldments

H. TSUGE, Y. TARUTANI, and T. KUDO\*

## Abstract

The effect of nitrogen, which is one of the most important elements in duplex stainless steels (SSs), was investigated for resistance to pitting corrosion and stress corrosion cracking (SCC) in various environments. The increase in nitrogen content increases the resistance of the base metal, which results from the improvement in the resistance of the γ-phase, which has lower chromium and molybdenum contents compared to α-phase. Nitrogen is an important element in sustaining the corrosion resistance of the heat affected zone (HAZ), which is heated to 1350 C or higher, because nitrogen promotes the formation of γ-phase during the cooling process after welding and avoids the precipitation of chromium carbides or nitrides at grain boundaries. When the nitrogen content is less than 0.08% in 22%Cr, 5.5%Ni, and 3%Mo base composition, the HAZ has little γ-phase to lead to the high susceptibility to intergranular corrosion and intergranular stress corrosion cracking (IGSCC) occurs even in a pure $C O _ { 2 } { - } C l ^ { - }$ environment containing no ${ \sf H } _ { 2 } { \sf S }$ Nitrogen has little effect on the precipitation behavior of the other phase in the base metal during aging heat treatment of around 700 C.

## Introduction

The recent search for new sources of hydrocarbons has led to the production from more severe environments. Such wells are deep and often constructed at offshore sites. The environments usually contain considerable amounts of carbon dioxide $( \mathsf { C O } _ { 2 } ) ,$ hydrogen sulfide (HS), and concentrated brine. Oil and gas production from these wells expose construction materials, such as tubulars and line pipes, to very severe corrosive conditions.

Low alloy steels are not suitable because inhibited systems are not desirable for offshore use or for the high temperature portion of the well. The corrosion resistance of highly alloyed materials, for example, duplex stainless steels (SSs), high nickel SSs, and nickel-base alloys have been evaluated for the purpose of selecting proper materials depending on the various well condition.1-6Among those highly alloyed materials, 22%Cr duplex SS line pipes have been widely used in the field that contains ${ \mathsf { C O } } _ { 2 }$ and chloride ions with small amounts of H₂S. Duplex SSs usually contain some amount of nitrogen to improve the corrosion and mechanical properties welded joints. Corrosion behaviors such as pitting, intergranular corrosion, and SCC of 22%Cr duplex SS have been investigated by many researchers.7,8 The effect of nitrogen on corrosion behavior, however, has not been clarified yet.

In this paper, the effect of nitrogen content on the corrosion resistance of 22%Cr duplex SSs was investigated with respect to the base metals, the low temperature HAZ, and the high temperature HAZ, which is heated to 1350 C or higher.

## Test Method

The pitting potential was measured potentiodynamically in 5% NaCl deaerated solution at 60 C at a sweep rate of 20 mV/min with a potentiostat. The (reference) SCE and the platinum counter electrode were used in this study. The test specimen, a disk 15 mm in diameter and 2 mm in thickness, was polished with No. 600 emery paper, washed with water and alcohol, and then degreased with acetone. The potential at which the anodic current density increases dramatically by the occurrence of pitting was adopted as the pitting potential (Vc’).

Pitting corrosion tests in $\mathsf { F e C l } _ { 3 }$ aerated solution were also conducted by the measurement of the CPT of the materials using 25 x 50 x 2 mm specimens polished with No. 600 emery paper. The Strauss test duration was 16 h. These specimens were 10 x 40 x 3 mm and polished with No. 320 emery paper.

## Discussion

It has been shown that nitrogen is beneficial in improving the pitting corrosion resistance of the base metal. This beneficial effect of nitrogen can be attributed to improving the pitting corrosion resistant of the austenite phase, as discussed below. Chromium and molybdenum contents in the ferrite and austenite phases are almost constant independent of nitrogen content, as shown in Figure 21. The austenite phase has \~21%Cr-2.5%Mo, while the ferrite phase has 23%Cr-3.5%Mo. Assuming that the pitting index equation is $\complement \pm$ 3Mo + 16N (as proposed in Reference 9) and that the nitrogen exists only in the austenite phase of 60%, as shown in Figure 1, the nitrogen content required to get the same pitting corrosion resistance in austenite phase as that in the ferrite phase is \~0.18%. Therefore, the pitting corrosion is thought to initiate at the austenite phase below the nítrogen content of 0.18% from this rough estimation. Figure 22 shows the microscopic observation of pitting initiation of the 22%Cr duplex SS in NaCi solution. In the alloys with nitrogen contents of $0 . 1 3 7 \%$ pitting corrosion is observed to initiate in austenite phase. When the nitrogen content increases to 0.164%, it can be seen that pitting occurs at random on the ferrite and austenite phases. The estimation mentioned above almost agrees with the observation.

## Conclusion

The effects of nitrogen content of 22%Cr duplex SS on the corrosion resistance of welds was investigated. The results obtained are as follows:

1. The increase in nitrogen content increases the resistance to pitting corrosion and SCC of the base metal, which results from the improvement in the resistance of austenite phase by the dissolved nitrogen itself.

2. Nitrogen has little effect on the intergranular corrosion at the low temperature HAZ in the Huey test

3. More than 0.12% nitrogen is required for the 22%Cr, 5.5%Ni, and 3%Mo base alloys to maintain the corrosion resistance at high temperature HAZ, which is heated to 1350 C or higher. When the nitrogen content is less than 0.077%, the HAZ, which contains little austenite phase, has the high susceptibility to intergranular corrosion and IGSCC.

## Abstract

The influence of grain boundary microstructure on the susceptibility of thermally treated (TT) Inconel Alloy 600 to intergranular attack (IGA) and stress corrosion cracking (SCC) in 30%NaOH + $1 0 \% N a _ { 2 } S O _ { 4 }$ at 350 C was investigated. Both mill-annealed (MA) and laboratory solution-annealed alloys were aged at 700 C to establish the effect of initial processing on the material's response to thermal treatment. Treatments of 1 h or more at 700 C greatly improved the SCC resistance of solution-annealed material but did not improve the performance of MA material containing a high density of intragranular carbides. The poor behavior of this material after thermal treatment was associated with the formation of small $M _ { 2 3 } C _ { 6 }$ carbides qrowing at a high angle to the boundaries and the development of these into cellular colonies. This is attributed to the use of a MA temperature that was too low to take into solution sufficient carbon for the development of semicontinuous grain boundary carbides during the subsequent TT process.
